What is SunsetPing?

SunsetPing tells landscape photographers whether the light is worth the drive. You type a city or ZIP code and get a score from 1 to 10, the local sunset time, a short viewing window, and one sentence on why. You can also get a free email the same day. Checking a score does not require an account.

How does the sunset score work?

The score is a whole number from 1 to 10. It uses the weather in the hour around sunset: low cloud, mid cloud, and high cloud, plus humidity, visibility, and precipitation. Enough broken cloud to catch the color scores higher. A thick low blanket, rain, or poor visibility scores lower. A completely clear sky also scores lower, because there is little for the light to catch. 8 or above means don't miss it. 6 or 7 is worth stepping outside. 4 or 5 is a quick peek. 3 or below means you can probably skip tonight. The same rules, shifted to sunrise, score the morning alerts.

When do alerts arrive?

The sunset alert arrives about three hours before sunset. Sunrise alerts arrive about an hour before sunrise when you turn them on. A day-before note arrives at 4:00 PM local about tomorrow when you turn that on.

Where are the city pages?

Each page includes tonight's sunset time, golden hour, and score in the HTML, from the same forecast the homepage uses. It also lists sunset time and golden-hour start for the next 7 days.
